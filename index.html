COMPOSE: Compositional Medical Phrase Grounding

Investigating whether chest X-ray grounding models follow the image and the complete clinical phrase—or rely on recurring anatomical priors.

Central question. Can a model achieve plausible localisation by exploiting recurring spatial patterns in the training set, without sufficiently using the test image or the complete clinical phrase? COMPOSE studies this possibility with explicit diagnostics for image dependence and compositional consistency.

Abstract

Medical phrase grounding localises radiological findings described in text within a corresponding image. Standard localisation metrics evaluate predicted boxes against reference annotations, but overlap alone does not establish whether a model's prediction is conditioned on the image, the pathology, the anatomy, and laterality together. We study this distinction in chest X-ray grounding, focusing on the possibility that recurring spatial patterns in the training data act as shortcuts. COMPOSE brings together training-set spatial-prior baselines, image-swap sensitivity, truth-checked phrase interventions, and left–right flip equivariance. We also investigate a displacement-based training objective and a Binding Score intended to summarise complementary aspects of localisation and image dependence. The project aims to make compositional grounding failures more observable and evaluation more informative than overlap metrics alone.

Problem overview

A phrase such as “right lower lobe consolidation” combines pathology, anatomical location, and laterality. A model should localise the described finding in the specific radiograph—not merely return a region that is often associated with that phrase or class.

Why evaluate compositionality?

01

Overlap is not the whole story

A predicted box may overlap a reference box because annotations are spatially concentrated. That overlap alone does not establish that the model used the visual evidence in the particular image.

02

The phrase has multiple components

Pathology, anatomy, and laterality jointly constrain the target. Changing one component should lead to a response consistent with the image and the validity of the modified phrase.

03

Groundability matters

Some phrases should be localised; others may be non-groundable or ambiguous. Evaluation should distinguish correct abstention from a system that abstains on everything.

04

Image dependence should be tested

Image swaps, controlled phrase interventions, and flip tests probe different behaviours that conventional box-overlap scores may not reveal.

Compositional diagnostic protocol

Four complementary tests designed to diagnose different potential shortcuts and inconsistencies.

1

Training-set spatial prior

Estimate a class-conditional mean box using training annotations only, then evaluate that fixed prior on held-out examples. Compare it with model performance under the same split and metric.

2

Image-swap sensitivity

Compare a matched image with an appropriate mismatched image. Measure whether localisation quality changes in response to the image, while controlling for class and phrase where possible.

3

Truth-checked phrase interventions

Change pathology, anatomy, or laterality while holding the image fixed. Evaluate only interventions with defensible targets; distinguish supported, contradicted, and ambiguous cases.

4

Left–right flip equivariance

Horizontally flip the image and switch left/right in the phrase. Check whether the predicted region transforms consistently with the image and the revised phrase.

COMPOSE

COMPOSE investigates training objectives and evaluation measures intended to complement standard phrase-grounding metrics.

Displacement-based objective

For valid paired phrases, compare the change in predicted box location with the corresponding change in reference location. The objective is intended to discourage predictions that remain fixed when a valid intervention requires a spatial change.

Binding Score

A composite score designed to bring together localisation quality, image-swap sensitivity, flip consistency, and resistance to confident predictions on contradicted phrases. Component metrics should also be reported separately.

Evaluation principle: no single score is sufficient evidence of genuine visual grounding. COMPOSE diagnostics should be interpreted jointly, with explicit degenerate baselines and held-out evaluation.

Data and evaluation

The study evaluates chest X-ray phrase grounding using benchmarks including MS-CXR and PadChest-GR, with Chest ImaGenome used for anatomical-region supervision and prior analysis. Patient-level separation and a frozen test set are important for interpreting spatial-prior and intervention results.
